Frailty in Chronic Kidney Disease: Why Serial Physical Performance Assessment Matters

An international consensus report highlights the importance of repeated frailty assessment, functional trajectories, and standardized physical performance measurement across the CKD pathway.

Frailty is highly prevalent in chronic kidney disease (CKD) and is associated with increased mortality, hospitalization, disability, and poorer quality of life. A 2026 international consensus report in Nature Reviews Nephrology emphasized that frailty in CKD should not be viewed as a static condition, but as a dynamic state that can worsen, stabilize, or improve over time.

The report was developed through an international consensus conference held in Hong Kong, involving 28 experts from 21 cities across five continents and representing nephrology, geriatrics, rehabilitation, palliative care, and nutrition.

Why serial assessment matters

One of the central messages of the consensus report is that frailty status can change over time.

Patients may transition between non-frail, pre-frail, and frail states, and some patients can recover functional capacity. For this reason, repeated assessment can provide more clinically meaningful information than a single baseline measurement.

The consensus group recommends:

  • routine frailty assessment in adults aged 65 years and older with CKD
  • assessment in younger patients with high-risk features such as sarcopenia or malnutrition
  • reassessment every 6–12 months
  • more frequent reassessment after hospitalization, acute kidney injury, dialysis initiation, transplantation, or other major clinical events
  • standardized timing of assessments in patients receiving haemodialysis

The report also notes that multiple time-point assessments are preferred when tracking frailty trajectory.

Frailty is a trajectory, not a single score

Longitudinal studies cited in the consensus report show that frailty status is not fixed.

In CKD and dialysis populations, patients can move between frailty states over time. Some patients deteriorate, while others improve. These transitions are clinically important because worsening frailty trajectories are associated with poorer outcomes, while improvement in frailty status has been associated with lower mortality and fewer hospitalizations.

The consensus therefore emphasizes the value of tracking frailty trajectories rather than relying only on a single time-point assessment.

The role of physical performance assessment

Frailty is multidimensional, and the consensus report recommends that frailty assessment should not rely on physical function tests alone.

However, physical performance remains an important component of frailty assessment and longitudinal monitoring.

The report specifically cites evidence on the Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB), one-legged standing test, and Timed Up & Go test in haemodialysis populations, including data on test–retest reliability and minimal detectable change.

It also stresses that physical performance measurements in dialysis patients can be influenced by acute physiological factors such as:

  • volume status
  • timing relative to dialysis
  • post-dialysis fatigue
  • electrolyte and fluid shifts

For this reason, standardized assessment conditions are important when repeated measurements are used.

Why standardization matters

Repeated functional assessment is only useful when measurements are comparable over time.

In CKD, this is particularly important because changes in physical performance may reflect both true changes in function and short-term physiological variation.

A standardized assessment protocol can help reduce measurement variability related to:

  • assessor technique
  • timing procedures
  • scoring methods
  • clinical setting
  • repeated follow-up visits

This becomes especially relevant when functional changes are monitored over months or years.

Where AndanteFit may fit

AndanteFit is an automated system for standardized SPPB assessment.

It provides a complete SPPB assessment in approximately three minutes and enables repeated measurements without manual timing or rater-dependent scoring.

In addition to the conventional 0–12 SPPB score, AndanteFit continuously acquires performance data during:

  • gait assessment
  • chair-stand testing
  • balance testing

This allows conventional SPPB scoring to be retained while also preserving the underlying continuous physical performance parameters.

For CKD and dialysis research, this type of standardized repeated assessment may be useful for monitoring physical function across different stages of care:

CKD follow-up → dialysis initiation → rehabilitation or exercise intervention → transplantation → post-treatment recovery

Potential research applications

The consensus report identifies several research questions that are directly relevant to longitudinal physical performance measurement. These include:

  • identifying the best tools for determining frailty trajectories in people with CKD under 65 years of age
  • determining the optimal timing of serial frailty screening
  • evaluating whether dynamic frailty trajectories provide better information than static baseline measures
  • defining the most appropriate outcome measures for interventions targeting frailty
  • evaluating physical function during rehabilitation, exercise, and prehabilitation

Standardized physical performance data may contribute to these research questions as one component of broader multidimensional frailty assessment.

An important distinction

AndanteFit is not intended to replace multidimensional frailty assessment.

The consensus report specifically recommends against using physical function tests alone to define frailty.

Instead, AndanteFit may be used as a standardized method for objectively measuring and longitudinally tracking the physical performance component of frailty assessment. The concept of frailty itself is covered separately.

Summary

The 2026 international consensus report on frailty in CKD reinforces three key principles:

  1. Frailty in CKD is dynamic and should be monitored over time.
  2. Repeated assessment should be standardized to improve comparability.
  3. Physical performance is an important component of frailty assessment, but should be interpreted within a broader multidimensional framework.

AndanteFit may provide a practical platform for standardized, repeated physical performance assessment using the SPPB, while also preserving continuous gait, chair-stand, and balance data for longitudinal analysis.
