Patients on lecanemab: look at physical function, not only brain pathology

A team led by Professor Sung Hoon Kang in the Department of Neurology at Korea University Guro Hospital analysed 187 patients with early Alzheimer's disease who started lecanemab (Leqembi) and published the results in Aging and Disease[1]. Patients who were physically frail or sarcopenic before treatment declined faster clinically during therapy, and frail patients had infusion-related reactions more often. Physical frailty was assessed with AndanteFit, an automated SPPB system.

The study

Patients on lecanemab are usually followed with cognitive testing, brain imaging and blood biomarkers. The team asked whether physical status — walking, balance and lower-limb function — was also related to how patients fared on treatment.

The analysis used K-LEARN, a prospective real-world cohort. It included 187 patients with early Alzheimer's disease who started lecanemab between December 2024 and May 2026. The team assessed physical frailty and sarcopenia before treatment, then tracked cognition and daily function, adverse events and blood biomarkers during therapy[1].

Physical frailty was classified with the SPPB, which tests balance, gait speed and rising from a chair. Sarcopenia was assessed with the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia 2019 criteria (AWGS 2019).

Roughly one in three patients starting treatment for early Alzheimer's disease already had sarcopenia.

Lower physical function, faster clinical decline

Patients with physical frailty or sarcopenia at baseline worsened faster on the Clinical Dementia Rating–Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) than those without. CDR-SB is a composite measure of cognition and everyday functioning.

The association held after adjustment for age, sex, education, APOE genotype and baseline clinical severity[1]. Two patients who start treatment at a similar cognitive level may still follow different courses depending on their physical status.

Infusion-related reactions more than twice as common in frail patients

Frail patients also had more infusion-related reactions.

Infusion-related reactions: 38.5% of physically frail patients versus 17.0% of non-frail patients. The association remained significant after adjustment for key clinical factors[1].

By contrast, changes in the blood biomarker p-tau217 did not differ significantly by frailty or sarcopenia status. The physical assessment picked up a difference that the blood biomarker did not.

How to read the findings

This was an observational study without an untreated control group. It does not show that frailty or sarcopenia changes the treatment effect of lecanemab itself[1]. The fair reading is that physical status is useful information for understanding the clinical course and tolerability of treatment.

What it means for memory clinics

The authors recommend assessing physical function alongside cognition, brain imaging and blood biomarkers during lecanemab therapy, and building it into each patient's care plan[1].

  • Baseline assessment: record the SPPB and a sarcopenia assessment with the cognitive, imaging and biomarker work-up
  • Individual care plans: watch infusion-related reactions and clinical course more closely in patients found to be frail
  • Repeat measurement: repeat the same test the same way during treatment to track change in physical function

To fit physical testing into a clinic's workflow, the measurement has to be quick and consistent across testers and visits. AndanteFit, used in this study, automates the balance, 4 m gait and five-times chair stand subtests of the SPPB. It removes manual stopwatch timing and records results quantitatively under a standardised procedure.

Physical function testing is not only for diagnosing sarcopenia and frailty. This study shows it can also inform the clinical course and tolerability of Alzheimer's disease treatment.
